Your two assessments

Both components are worth 50% each, both must be passed at 40%+, and there is no compensation between them.

50%
Component 1

Practical assessments — simulated placement

Must pass (40%+) Exempt from late penalty Clinical assessment

During simulation week you'll complete practical assessments in realistic scenarios. You're being assessed on how you bring together four things at once:

  • Clinical reasoning — thinking through the "why" behind what you do.
  • Communication — with clients and colleagues.
  • Practical skills — safe, effective hands-on practice.
  • Professional conduct — working within regulatory frameworks and professional boundaries.
Students will complete practical assessments during a simulated placement exploring their ability to integrate clinical reasoning, communication, practical skills, and professional conduct within realistic scenarios. Competence is demonstrated not only through safe and effective practice but also through adherence to regulatory frameworks and professional boundaries that safeguard both clients and practitioners (50%).
50%
Component 2

Written reflective piece — 1,000 words maximum

Must pass (40%+) Late penalty applies Submit via CANVAS by 11am

A short reflective piece of work (1,000 words maximum) with two jobs to do:

  • Explore your experiences of occupational therapy (your simulation week and PP1 placement give you the raw material).
  • Consider your learning and development needs — to support your continual professional development in line with professional standards.
Students will present a short reflective piece of work (1000 words maximum) exploring experiences of occupational therapy and considering their learning and development needs to support continual professional development in line with professional standards (50%).

Formative assessment (no marks — but don't skip it)

You complete a one-week practice placement (PP1, 37.5 hours) in a health or social care setting. It's formatively assessed — meaning you get feedback rather than a mark — and it sits after simulation week and before your reflective submission, making it ideal material for Component 2.

😟 What happens if I don't pass? (Re-sit strategy)

The format for the re-assessment will be the same as for the first attempt, with two specifics:

1. The requirements for each station will be different to the first sitting.
2. Students would submit a re-worked version of their reflection.

You won't be left to figure it out alone: guidance is given before the second attempt through a 1:1 re-sit assessment tutorial with a member of the module teaching team, used to identify knowledge and skills gaps and agree actions to address them — to best facilitate a successful re-sit attempt.

⚠️ Could the assessment plan change?

In exceptional circumstances (e.g. global pandemics), plans for assessment may be subject to change. Contact will be made via the VLE and an e-mail will be sent to each student informing them of any changes that prove to be necessary. The Health and Life Sciences FAQSC will be informed in advance of any such changes, so that approval can be given.

Learning outcomes

These four outcomes are what the module — and its assessments — are designed to evidence. Use them as a lens when preparing: every station and every paragraph of your reflection should map back to these.

LO1

Describe the core roles, responsibilities, and values of the occupational therapy profession.

Think: What does an OT actually do, and what values underpin the profession?

LO2

Identify the key aspects of HCPC regulation, local governance and basic legal/ethical principles that shape safe and effective practice.

Think: Who regulates you, and what rules keep clients and practitioners safe?

LO3

Demonstrate emerging professional communication across a range of simulated environments.

Think: This one is shown live — in simulation week scenarios.

LO4

Describe the importance of reflection and continued professional development.

Think: This is the heart of your written reflective piece.

Reading the verbs
At Level 4 the verbs matter: describe and identify ask you to show clear foundational knowledge; demonstrate asks you to show a skill in action. Nobody expects mastery yet — the brief itself says "emerging professional communication".

Presentation & communication requirements

Your written work must:

  • Follow academic integrity guidelines
  • Use clear academic writing with accurate grammar and spelling
  • Reference all sources using Harvard ('Cite Them Right') style
  • Be written consistently in the third person
Third person + reflection?
Yes — an unusual combination that catches people out. Reflective writing here should still be in the third person (e.g. "the student observed…" rather than "I observed…"). If in doubt, check with the module team.

About the module

OCCU182 is a Level 4, 15-credit module — the first in the Personal and Professional Development theme.

What the module introduces

  • The history of the occupational therapy profession
  • An introduction to the professional values, behaviours and attitudes required of an occupational therapist
  • The role of the regulatory and professional bodies
  • An introduction to the code of conduct and professional practice for occupational therapists
  • An introduction to continuing professional development and the professional requirements

You will also begin to develop a range of skills required for professional practice, including professional communication skills, leadership and readiness for clinical placements — engaging in a simulated placement week and a one-week clinical placement (PP1), which will be formatively assessed.

Indicative assessment content

The topics your learning — and assessment — will draw on:

  • History of occupational therapy profession
  • Understanding of professional values, behaviours and attitude and professionalism
  • Professional standards, ethics, code of conduct, Professional Identity
  • Introduction to service awareness, leadership and placement readiness
  • Development of therapeutic communication
  • Development of observational skills, communication skills, therapeutic and professional relationships
  • Introduction to personal development tutorials
  • KnowHow Tutorials
  • ELFH / Oliver McGowan training
  • Simulation week and including a one-week placement (formatively assessed)

How you'll be taught

The module is delivered through digital case-based and simulation resources, with contents structured and scaffolded to build confidence, encourage independence and promote mastery. As one of the core clinical modules in the programme, it is designed to directly develop practical clinical knowledge and skills for the future workforce through clinical placement and authentic clinical learning experiences employing key signature pedagogies: case-based learning (CBL), practical clinical skills, placement and simulation. Learning communities will be established through small group activities during tutorials and practical clinical sessions incorporating supported discussion and peer-supported learning tasks.

🏛️ Seminars

Synchronous online or face-to-face, recorded for asynchronous access.

Large classroom-style learning and teaching sessions providing the opportunity for small group working and gaining feedback from peers and the tutor. In this way students learn from others' thinking and experience. They also provide opportunity for question and answer sessions to clarify concepts and issues being taught. Briefing and de-briefing for practice placement will be conducted within seminars.

👥 Tutorials

Synchronous online or face-to-face, recorded for asynchronous access.

Small group discussion in uni-professional groups, and an academic advisor meeting conducted online. Engaging in tutorials can highlight strengths and identify areas for further study and areas for professional development needed by the student.

📚 Self-directed study

Students will consolidate learning through self-directed study and will be guided towards appropriate study resources to build their learning.

🏥 Practice placement

Placement completed under the supervision of a clinical practice educator; the student is given the opportunity to apply skills and knowledge within the clinical environment at the appropriate level of study.

💻 On-line test

Completion of e-learning for health mandatory training modules.

🎭 Simulation

Practice of skills required for clinical practice within the university environment or delivered virtually on-line.
